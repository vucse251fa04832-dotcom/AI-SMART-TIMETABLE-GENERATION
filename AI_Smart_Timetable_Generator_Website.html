01 · Problem & Motivation

Scheduling is a constraint problem.

Why manual scheduling is difficult

Creating a college timetable manually is complex and time-consuming because faculty availability, subjects, sections, classrooms, working days, periods, and weekly subject requirements must all be coordinated.

Manual scheduling can lead to faculty conflicts, classroom conflicts, section conflicts, uneven subject distribution, and assignments outside availability.

What this project does

The proposed system uses AI-based constraint planning to automatically generate a feasible timetable while satisfying the required scheduling constraints.

Feasible scheduling · Constraint-aware
02 · Objectives

From requirements to a usable schedule.

03 · Key Idea

Constraint Satisfaction Problem

Variables

Session assignments that require a subject, faculty, section, day, period, and classroom.

Domains

Possible combinations of available days, periods, and classrooms.

Constraints

Hard and soft rules that guide the scheduling search.

Search

Backtracking and constraint checking explore assignments until a feasible timetable is found.

Key callout: AI-based constraint planning helps generate feasible schedules while automatically detecting and avoiding major timetable conflicts.

06 · Constraints

Hard rules first. Quality second.

Hard Constraints · Must Satisfy

  • A faculty member cannot teach two classes at the same time.
  • A classroom cannot be used by two classes simultaneously.
  • A section cannot attend two subjects simultaneously.
  • Every subject must receive its required number of classes per week.
  • Faculty and classroom availability must be respected where data exists.

Soft Constraints · Optimize

  • Avoid too many consecutive classes for a faculty member.
  • Distribute subjects across different days.
  • Minimize unnecessary free periods.
  • Avoid excessive consecutive difficult/major subjects where applicable.
  • Balance faculty workload where possible.
